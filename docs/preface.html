Preface

Agricultural research produces data from field experiments, surveys, laboratory assays and long-term monitoring. Turning those data into sound conclusions needs two things: a clear understanding of the statistical method, and the practical skill to carry it out on a computer. Most students learn the first in their theory courses but struggle with the second. This book is meant to close that gap.

How this book is organised

The chapters follow the five units of the STAT 531 syllabus (see the Syllabus):

  • Getting started (Chapters 1–6): statistical software, installing R and RStudio, R basics, packages, importing data and built-in data sets.
  • Unit I – Describing data (Chapters 7–9): summarisation and tabulation, descriptive statistics, graphs and exploratory data analysis.
  • Unit II – Distributions and tests of hypotheses (Chapters 10–12): fitting distributions and testing goodness of fit, large-sample tests, and t, F and chi-square tests.
  • Unit III – Analysis of variance and covariance (Chapters 13–16): one-way ANOVA, multiple comparisons, standard experimental designs, factorial and split-plot experiments, contrasts and ANCOVA.
  • Unit IV – Mixed models and regression (Chapters 17–18): mixed models, variance components, correlation and regression.
  • Unit V – Multivariate methods, non-linear models and time series (Chapters 19–23).

Each chapter states the statistical model, shows the R code, explains how to read the output, and ends with exercises.

Conventions

R code is shown in grey boxes. Lines of output start with #>, so you can copy a whole box into R without editing:

mean(c(12, 15, 18))
#> [1] 15

Function names are written with parentheses, e.g. aov(), and package names in code font, e.g. agricolae. Notes and tips are highlighted like this:

A note or tip contains practical advice, common mistakes or a pointer to further reading.

Software and packages

The book was built with R version 4.3.3 (2024-02-29). The following packages are used. Install them once with:

install.packages(c(
  "agricolae", "agridat", "car", "emmeans", "multcomp", "lme4", "lmerTest",
  "fitdistrplus", "vcd", "HistData", "psych", "FactoMineR", "factoextra",
  "forecast", "TTR", "modeest", "pastecs", "readxl", "corrplot", "nortest",
  "lmtest"
))

MASS comes with every R installation.

Prerequisites

You should be familiar with basic statistical theory. For the theory behind the methods in this book, please refer to my e-book Textbook of Agricultural Statistics and to standard texts such as Goon, Gupta, and Dasgupta (1983), Gupta and Kapoor (1997), Snedecor and Cochran (1989) and Gomez and Gomez (1984).
